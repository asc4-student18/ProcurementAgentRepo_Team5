REQ-015 is intentionally ambiguous in the fixture, but escalating is the better default for this implementation because the request leaves the cost center in a tight-budget state after the purchase. Even though the request is currently within budget and no explicit policy violation is triggered, post-purchase remaining funds become a small fraction of the quarterly envelope, which increases operational risk for later mandatory spend. In procurement operations, this is a classic case where strict pass/fail policy checks are not enough and a judgment call is required to prevent avoidable budget pressure.

Escalation also aligns with the control objectives of this project: transparent rationale, conservative governance, and predictable handling of edge cases. The agent now treats tight-budget scenarios as a structured escalation signal, which avoids silent approvals in borderline situations while still preserving deny behavior for explicit policy breaches. This design is auditable and explainable to reviewers because the rationale explicitly flags the low remaining budget condition, and it keeps human decision authority in the loop for cases where business context may justify approval despite elevated budget sensitivity.
